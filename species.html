Dipterocarpaceae · Vatica

Vatica borneensis

A tree endemic to Borneo's dipterocarp forests, this species can grow to about 35 metres. It has leathery elliptic leaves and dense clusters of pinkish-brown flowers.

Conservation: Near Threatened

Explore on iNaturalist ↗ Specimen image: Harvard University Herbaria
02

Dipterocarpaceae · Vatica

Vatica flavida

Known as resak padi, this evergreen tree is native to Peninsular Malaysia. It bears yellow flowers and grows in wet tropical lowland forest, including swampy habitats.

Conservation: Critically Endangered

Dipterocarpaceae · Dipterocarpus

Dipterocarpus alatus

A large Southeast Asian rainforest tree recognised by its straight trunk, resin and two-winged fruits. It often grows in lowland forest and near rivers.

Genus: Dipterocarpus

Explore on iNaturalist ↗ Photo: Wangworn Sankamethawee / iNaturalist · CC BY-NC
04

Dipterocarpaceae · Dipterocarpus

Dipterocarpus grandiflorus

This medium to large hardwood tree occurs across South and Southeast Asia. Its timber is sold as keruing, while its gum has traditionally been used as a waterproofing varnish.

Common name: Alakal

Lauraceae · Actinodaphne

Actinodaphne mushaensis

An evergreen laurel native to Taiwan, this plant carries glossy leaves in clusters near the ends of its branches. Its small flowers and drupes are typical of the genus.

Range: Taiwan

Lauraceae · Actinodaphne

Actinodaphne wightiana

This evergreen tree is associated with the forests of southern India. Aromatic foliage and compact flower clusters identify it as a member of the laurel family.

Range: Southern India

Lauraceae · Endiandra

Endiandra sieberi

Commonly called hard corkwood, this evergreen rainforest tree grows along Australia's eastern coast. It develops dark fleshy fruits that contain a single large seed.

The domatia tree is an eastern Australian rainforest species. Tiny sheltered pockets where leaf veins meet provide the feature behind its common name.

Burseraceae · Canarium

Burseraceae · Dacryodes

Dacryodes excelsa

This tall Caribbean forest tree is commonly called gommier. Its pale aromatic resin was traditionally used for torches, incense and sealing wooden boats.

Myristicaceae · Horsfieldia

Horsfieldia irya

This nutmeg relative grows in tropical Asian lowland and riverine forests. Its fruit opens to display a dark seed partly covered by a colourful aril.
